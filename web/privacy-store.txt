## Privacy Policy

Version 1 · Effective [date] · Last updated [month year]

This is a free Fire TV app that shows the aircraft over your home. This policy explains what the app does with information about you. It is the whole policy; there are no hidden parts.

## The short version

• We run no server. The app has no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and it sends nothing to us.

• Your home location, settings and aircraft history are stored only on your TV.

• To show planes and maps, your TV asks public services for data. They get your location rounded to about 5 km (3 miles), map requests for that area, what you type into search, and the flight numbers and codes of nearby aircraft. Each one also sees your TV's internet (IP) address. All of them are listed below.

• The app asks before it sends anything.

• Settings › Privacy › Erase all data deletes everything the app stored. Uninstalling does too.

• We only hear from you if you email us or send a tip.

• We don't sell personal information, use it for ads, or use it to train AI.

## What the app uses, and why

The app never asks Fire TV for your location, and never uses a microphone, camera or contacts. It works only with what you give it.

• Your home location. After you agree on the "Before we start" screen, the app makes a first guess at your city from your internet address. You confirm it, or search for a city, ZIP code or street address, and you can nudge the spot on the map. The app uses this location to find aircraft near you, draw the map, and work out your time zone and elevation.

• What you type into search. It goes to the place-search services below, which turn it into a map position.

• Nearby aircraft. The app downloads the positions of aircraft near you, then looks up each flight's route by its flight number, and a photo of the aircraft on the main card by its code or registration. Aircraft broadcast this information publicly and it isn't about you, but the requests do show roughly where you are.

• Your aircraft history. A log of what the app has seen near you, so it can show today's planes, records and recent sightings.

• Your preferences, such as layout, units and map size.

Aircraft model photos, fonts and sounds come inside the app. Showing or playing them sends nothing. Photos of the exact aircraft come from Planespotters.net, listed below.

## What's stored on your TV

All of this is kept in the app's private storage on your Fire TV. None of it is sent to us.

• Your place and settings: the place you chose (its name, region, country, exact coordinates, time zone and elevation, and the street address if you typed one) and your preferences. Kept until you change them or erase them.

• Your agreement: that you pressed Agree and continue, with the date and the policy version. Kept until you erase it.

• Today's log: aircraft seen today, up to 4,000 (how close each came, its type, flight number and origin), a list of up to 3,000 aircraft codes seen today, tonight's cargo flights, today's military aircraft (aircraft code and type, up to 500), today's records, and an all-time count of aircraft types. Today's part resets at midnight, and moving your location more than about 2 miles (3 km) clears the log.

• Recent sightings: up to the last 20 aircraft in your alert zone (aircraft code, flight number, registration, type and time), of which the newest 5 are shown. Newer sightings replace older ones. Cleared when you move your location more than about 2 miles (3 km), or switch the alert zone between All around and Window view.

• Saved routes: which airports a flight number flies between, kept for up to 24 hours, and for 6 hours the position, height and time of aircraft that passed through your alert zone, so their routes can be checked again.

• Slow-down notes: if adsb.lol or Planespotters.net asks the app to slow down, the time when it may ask again. Nothing about you.

• Saved maps: map data around your home, including your home point: up to two base maps (your current and previous map sizes) and up to three detailed street maps, with a list of which ones are saved. Refreshed after 30 days.

Your TV's web engine may also keep temporary copies of satellite pictures and aircraft photos. The app uses no cookies. Anyone who uses your TV can open Settings and see your saved place, so if that matters to you, pick a nearby town instead of your street.

## Services that receive data from your TV

To work, your TV sends requests straight to the services below. We have no contracts with them, and we never see what they receive. Each one handles it under its own privacy policy; what they say about logs is summarized here as of [month year].

Every service also receives your TV's IP address and the time of each request, plus standard technical details. Most requests carry a label with the app's name and version and our email address, so a service can reach us if something goes wrong. Aircraft photos and satellite pictures are loaded by your TV's web engine, which sends its usual browser details instead. None of these details identify you. The app can only reach the web addresses on a fixed list, and only over encrypted HTTPS.

adsb.fi (live aircraft positions)

• Receives: your location rounded to a grid of about 5 km (3 miles), and the size of the area to search.

• When: every 5 to 15 seconds while the app is on screen, more often on smaller maps. It pauses when you switch to another app. When no aircraft are nearby, a wider check at most every 2 hours, and again after you change the map size, alert zone or place.

• Who: adsb.fi, a community flight-tracking network. Its privacy policy doesn't name who runs it or where its servers are.

• Their policy: https://adsb.fi/privacy. It says adsb.fi collects the IP address, internet provider, browser type, operating system, device type and time of access, and may use outside providers for content delivery and traffic management. It doesn't say how long it keeps these.

adsb.lol (flight routes)

• Receives: the flight number of an aircraft near you. No location.

• When: at most one lookup every 5 seconds, and each answer is kept for 24 hours.

• Who: adsb.lol, a community project with servers at Netcup in Germany. Route lookups are served by GitHub through Cloudflare.

• Their policy: https://www.adsb.lol/docs/privacy-license. It says temporary logs are kept for under 30 days.

Planespotters.net (aircraft photos)

• Receives: the code, and if needed the registration, of the aircraft on the main card, then requests for its photo. No location.

• When: at most one lookup every 1.5 seconds. Answers are kept in memory for 24 hours and never saved.

• Who: Planespotters.net, Berlin, Germany, and its image servers.

• Their policy: https://www.planespotters.net/legal/privacypolicy. It says system logs may include the IP address and are kept as long as their purpose requires, without a set period, and that it uses Cloudflare.

OpenFreeMap (the street map)

• Receives: requests for map tiles covering the area around the same rounded point. They don't contain your coordinates, but they show the area you're in.

• When: once for each location and map size, then again after 30 days.

• Who: Hyperknot Software Kft., Hungary, served through Cloudflare.

• Their policy: https://openfreemap.org/privacy/. It says access logs don't include IP addresses, error logs may include your IP address and the map file requested, and logs are deleted after 7 days, or up to 30 days while a security problem is investigated.

U.S. Geological Survey, The National Map (satellite pictures, US only)

• Receives: requests for satellite tiles around the same rounded point.

• When: each time a satellite layout draws its map.

• Who: the U.S. federal government.

• Their policy: https://www.usgs.gov/privacy

EOX (satellite pictures outside the US)

• Receives: requests for satellite tiles around the same rounded point.

• When: only in the Atlas Satellite layout, when your place is outside the US, each time it draws a new view.

• Who: EOX IT Services GmbH, Austria.

• Their policy: https://eox.at/privacy-notice. It doesn't say what their map servers log or for how long.

Open-Meteo (city and ZIP code search, time zone and elevation)

• Receives: what you type into search, after a short pause, unless it looks like a street address. And when you pick a street address, that place's rounded location, to get its time zone and elevation.

• Who: Open-Meteo, Switzerland.

• Their terms: https://open-meteo.com/en/terms. They say logs may include coordinates and are deleted after 90 days.

U.S. Census Bureau Geocoder (US street addresses)

• Receives: the street address you typed.

• When: only when you press Search and what you typed looks like a US street address.

• Who: the U.S. federal government.

• Their policy: https://www.census.gov/about/policies/privacy/privacy-policy.html. It says logs may be kept indefinitely.

OpenStreetMap Nominatim (other address searches)

• Receives: what you typed, up to 120 characters.

• When: only when you press Search, and only if what you typed looks like a street address outside the US, or is an address or postcode the other services couldn't find.

• Who: the OpenStreetMap Foundation, UK.

• Their policy: https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy. It says search queries may be analyzed to find missing addresses and postcodes, and that its logs are temporary, without giving a period.

GeoJS (the first city guess)

• Receives: only your IP address; that is the whole request.

• When: right after you press Agree and continue, and again each time the app starts until you confirm a place. After an erase, the same applies once you agree again.

• Who: GeoJS, Australia, running on Cloudflare and Hetzner and using MaxMind GeoLite2 location data.

• Their policy: https://geojs.io/privacy/. It says GeoJS keeps no access logs, only error logs.

## Deleting your data, and changing your mind

• Settings › Privacy › Erase all data deletes everything under "What's stored on your TV" at once, stops all requests, and takes the app back to the "Before we start" screen. That also withdraws your permission: nothing is sent until you agree again.

• Moving your location more than about 2 miles (3 km) clears today's log and your recent sightings.

• Uninstalling removes everything the app stored, and works even if the app won't open. On Fire TV: Settings › Applications › Manage Installed Applications, then pick this app and choose Uninstall. Menu names vary by model.

• What we can't delete: the logs the services above keep. We have no access to them, so use the links above, or email us and we'll help.

• What we hold: emails you send us, and tip records. Email us to see them or have them deleted. We'll delete everything except the tip records the law requires us to keep.

## How long things are kept

• On your TV: as described above, or until you erase or uninstall.

• By the services above: as their policies say.

• Emails to us: deleted within 12 months of our last exchange, unless we need them for a dispute or a legal duty.

• Tip records: 7 years, for tax and accounting.

## Tips through Ko-fi

The app is free. If you like, you can tip us with the Ko-fi QR code in Settings. Tips are optional and unlock nothing.

The QR code is made on the TV, and the TV sends nothing to Ko-fi. If you scan it and tip, Ko-fi (Ko-fi Labs Limited, UK) and its payment processors handle the payment, and we receive:

• from Ko-fi: the name you choose to give, your email address and any message you write;

• from PayPal or Stripe, depending on how you pay: possibly your legal name and email, and for card payments through Stripe, your billing address and limited card details such as the card type and last four digits. We never receive full card or bank numbers.

Depending on what you choose on Ko-fi, your name and message may also appear publicly on our Ko-fi page. We use this information to thank you, handle refunds or questions, and keep tax records. We don't use it for marketing or add you to any list.

Privacy policies: Ko-fi https://more.ko-fi.com/privacy · PayPal https://www.paypal.com/us/legalhub/privacy-full · Stripe https://stripe.com/privacy

## Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. A parent can erase everything at any time with Settings › Privacy › Erase all data. If you think a child has emailed us or sent a tip, tell us and we'll delete it.

## Security

• Every connection uses encrypted HTTPS, and plain connections are refused.

• The app can only contact the services on its fixed list.

• The app doesn't download code; everything it runs is part of the app.

• There are no accounts or passwords to steal, and we hold almost nothing about you.

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a breach affecting information we hold, we'll tell you as the law requires.

## Your rights

Wherever you live, you can:

• see what the app stored (your place is in Settings, and your history is on screen);

• change it by picking a new location;

• delete it with Erase all data, or by uninstalling;

• withdraw your permission with Erase all data;

• ask us for a copy of the emails and tip records we hold, or ask us to correct or delete them.

The app's data never reaches us, so we can't look it up or delete it for you. If you ask, we'll confirm that we hold none of it and show you how to manage it on the TV. We won't treat you differently for using any of these rights.

## If you own or fly an aircraft

The app shows only what aircraft broadcast publicly and what adsb.fi, adsb.lol and Planespotters.net publish: aircraft code, flight number, registration, type, position and photos. It doesn't show owner names. Sightings are kept only on the viewer's TV. If you'd like your aircraft hidden in the app, email us its registration, and we'll consider adding it to a hide list in a future update.
